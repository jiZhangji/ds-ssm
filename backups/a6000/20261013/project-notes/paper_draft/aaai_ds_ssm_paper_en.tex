\begin{abstract}
Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) target detection is difficult because weak target responses coexist with multiplicative speckle and strong background scattering. Visual state space models offer linear-complexity global modeling, but an unconstrained scan may also propagate clutter-dominated evidence across image tokens. We propose the \methodfull{} (\method), a lightweight SAR-aware module that modifies the input drive of a selective state space block. It attenuates local sequence residuals and uses a Perceptual Feature Importance Module (PFIM) to estimate token importance from local trend and two-dimensional variation. The estimate factorizes scan-drive control into energy-aware damping and importance-centered scaling, while separately adapting the transition step size. A numerically stable CUDA implementation performs selective scan in FP32 and replaces only one shallow Mamba-YOLO stage, yielding a 5.96M-parameter, 13.21-GFLOP T-scale detector. In one matched 300-epoch OGSOD-1.0 run per architecture, PFIM-T improves AP$_{75}$ by 0.760 and \apcoco{} by 1.300 percentage points over Mamba-YOLO-T, while changing \apfifty{} by only 0.090 points and slightly reducing model complexity. Recovered SSDD, SAR-ShipDataset, and SAR-AIRcraft experiments provide directionally compatible archive observations, but are not used to establish a cross-dataset trend because their baselines or splits are not fully controlled. Results on densely packed tanks also reveal a limitation: improved aggregate localization does not imply uniform class-wise gains. The evidence therefore supports \method{} as a compact localization-oriented SAR state controller for the reported matched setting, rather than a universally superior detector.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

SAR sensors operate independently of daylight and are robust to adverse weather, making them important for maritime surveillance, airport monitoring, and emergency response. Their coherent imaging process, however, produces speckle and highly non-Gaussian background responses. Ships are often small and sparse, while aircraft are represented by disconnected scattering centers rather than stable optical contours. Metal buildings, coastlines, wakes, and airport infrastructure can therefore resemble true targets.

Modern YOLO detectors provide attractive efficiency, but conventional convolution does not explicitly model long-range structure. State space models (SSMs), including Mamba, offer linear-time sequence modeling through input-dependent selective scans \citep{mamba,s4}. Mamba-YOLO adapts this idea to object detection and establishes a compact SSM-based detector \citep{mambayolo}. Yet applying a generic visual SSM directly to SAR features introduces a specific failure mode: local clutter can enter the recurrent drive and influence many later tokens along the scan order.

Existing SAR-specific methods attack this problem from different directions. DiffusionDet generates boxes through iterative denoising \citep{diffusiondet}; DiffDet4SAR and MaDiNet adapt diffusion-based detection to SAR imagery \citep{diffdet4sar,madinet}. MaDiNet further introduces a large MambaSAR module and reports strong performance on SSDD, SAR-ShipDataset, and SAR-AIRcraft1.0. Its accuracy demonstrates the value of global context, while its complex diffusion pipeline motivates a complementary question: can SAR-specific state control improve a much smaller one-stage detector?

We answer this question with \method. The module uses a depthwise local sequence filter to estimate a background trend and attenuate local residual disturbances before the selective scan. PFIM then combines the filtered trend with two-dimensional local standard deviation to estimate token importance. It parameterizes two factors of the scan drive, an energy-conditioned damping factor $\gamma$ and an importance-centered factor $w$, and separately adapts the transition step $\Delta$. In the reported CUDA implementation, $\gamma$ and $w$ are not distinct recurrent operator locations: their product scales the scan input. We state this explicitly to keep the paper aligned with the executable code.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a residual-suppressed selective state space drive that attenuates local sequence disturbances before global propagation.
  \item We design PFIM to condition a factorized scan drive and transition-step adaptation on local trend and spatial-variation cues.
  \item We integrate one \method{} stage into a lightweight Mamba-YOLO detector, retaining 5.96M parameters and 13.21 GFLOPs.
\item We provide a matched, single-run OGSOD-1.0 baseline comparison and separate it from protocol-mismatched archive evidence on SSDD, SAR-ShipDataset, and SAR-AIRcraft.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{SAR Target Detection}

SSDD introduced an early public benchmark for SAR ship detection \citep{ssdd2017}, followed by an official standardized release and analysis \citep{ssddofficial}. SAR-ShipDataset contains 39,729 image patches from Gaofen-3 and Sentinel-1 under diverse coastal and open-sea conditions \citep{sarshipdataset}. SAR-AIRcraft1.0 contains 4,368 high-resolution images and seven fine-grained aircraft categories \citep{saraircraft}. OGSOD-1.0 was introduced as an optical-guided SAR benchmark and covers bridges, harbors, and oil tanks \citep{ogsod}. These datasets differ substantially in target density, image scale, and background complexity, making direct comparison sensitive to split and preprocessing choices.

One-stage YOLO models are widely used for efficient SAR detection \citep{yolov5,yolov8}. Diffusion-based detectors instead formulate localization as box denoising. DiffDet4SAR specializes this paradigm for aircraft, while MaDiNet combines diffusion with a MambaSAR backbone to model target-background context \citep{diffdet4sar,madinet}. Our objective is not to reproduce MaDiNet's diffusion pipeline; it is to inject SAR priors directly into a compact selective scan.

\subsection{Visual State Space Models}

Structured SSMs provide an alternative to quadratic self-attention \citep{s4}. Mamba makes transition parameters input dependent and introduces hardware-aware selective scan kernels \citep{mamba}. Mamba-YOLO builds an object detector around visual state space blocks and shows that an SSM backbone can be trained without large-scale pretraining \citep{mambayolo}. These generic blocks do not distinguish target scattering from speckle. \method{} addresses this limitation by controlling the scan input with local residual and importance cues.

\subsection{Design Objective and Placement}

A generic visual SSM treats every token as a valid state-space drive. This is undesirable for SAR imagery: an isolated speckle response can enter the scan and influence many subsequent positions, whereas aggressively suppressing all high-frequency responses can erase weak targets. We therefore separate two decisions: (i) whether a local response is inconsistent with its neighborhood, and (ii) whether that response is perceptually important. The first is handled by a learnable residual suppressor and the second by PFIM.

Given a shallow feature map $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C\times H\times W}$, we flatten and normalize it into
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}=\mathrm{LN}(\mathrm{Flatten}(\mathbf{F}))
  \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times N\times C},\qquad N=HW.
\end{equation}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}, only the P2/4 VSS stage of Mamba-YOLO is replaced by \method. P3--P5, the bidirectional feature pyramid, and the detection head remain unchanged. This placement controls noisy low-level evidence before repeated multi-scale fusion while avoiding the cost of replacing every backbone stage.

\subsection{Local Residual Suppression}

We estimate a channel-wise local trend over the flattened sequence using a depthwise 1-D convolution initialized as a three-tap mean filter:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{x}}=\mathrm{DWConv1D}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad
  \mathbf{r}=\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}.
\end{equation}
The state-space drive is de-biased by
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}-\lambda_s\mathbf{r},
  \label{eq:despeckle}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_s$ and the depthwise kernel are learned. Equation~\ref{eq:despeckle} is deliberately residual: it attenuates locally inconsistent responses instead of performing irreversible image-level despeckling. The block-level skip path still carries the original feature, allowing optimization to recover target evidence when suppression is excessive.

\subsection{Perceptual Feature Importance Module}

Sequence residual magnitude alone cannot distinguish speckle from a genuine scattering center. PFIM restores the spatial layout of $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ and computes the local first and second moments with a $3\times3$ average pool:
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol{\mu}&=\mathrm{AvgPool}_{2D}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}),\\
  \mathbf{s}&=\sqrt{\mathrm{AvgPool}_{2D}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^2)-\boldsymbol{\mu}^2+\epsilon}.
\end{align}
After flattening $\mathbf{s}$, PFIM combines spatial variation with the sequence trend:
\begin{equation}
  I_t=\sigma\!\left(\mathbf{W}_2\,\mathrm{GELU}
  \left(\mathbf{W}_1\mathrm{LN}([\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t;\mathbf{s}_t])\right)\right),
  \quad \widehat I_t=I_t-0.5.
  \label{eq:pfim}
\end{equation}
The last projection is zero-initialized, giving the non-saturated value $I_t=0.5$ at initialization. Because $\widehat I_t$ enters both scan-drive factors, PFIM contributes zero initial offset to them. The transition branch in Eq.~\ref{eq:delta}, however, uses the uncentered $I_t$ and therefore receives a small positive offset $\eta/2$. Thus the importance estimator starts non-saturated, but the complete module is not claimed to be an exact identity initialization.

\subsection{Factorized Drive and Transition Control}

The de-speckled tokens are projected into a scan drive and output gate,
\begin{equation}
  (\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z})=\mathrm{Split}(\mathbf{W}_{in}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}),
\end{equation}
while $\mathbf{B}_t$ and $\mathbf{C}_t$ are generated from $\mathbf{u}_t$. PFIM acts at two executable locations: it adapts the transition step and factorizes the scan-drive scale into two differently conditioned terms. The transition term is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t=\mathrm{softplus}(\mathbf{W}_{\Delta}\mathbf{u}_t+\eta I_t).
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
The first drive factor combines normalized trend energy $e_t=\lVert\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t\rVert_2/\sqrt{C}$ with centered importance:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_t=g_{min}+(1-g_{min})
  \sigma(-\alpha\sigma_n e_t+\beta\widehat I_t).
  \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
The floor $g_{min}=0.5$ prevents complete erasure. The second drive factor depends only on centered importance:
\begin{equation}
  w_t=\max\!\left(0.1,1+\tanh(\rho)\widehat I_t\right).
  \label{eq:write}
\end{equation}
Thus, $\gamma_t$ introduces energy-aware damping, $w_t$ rescales target-like current evidence, and $\Delta_t$ changes the effective temporal scale of the SSM. The first two terms are independently parameterized but multiply the same scan input in the executed model; they must not be interpreted as separate recurrent update sites.

\subsection{Ablation Variants}

To attribute the localization change to a specific mechanism, the same block is instantiated in several control forms. \texttt{no\_pfim} disables the importance network, leaving residual suppression and the energy-conditioned damping floor in Eq.~\ref{eq:gamma}; the importance-centered factor is set to one and the transition step in Eq.~\ref{eq:delta} receives no importance offset. It is therefore a reduced-parameter control rather than a capacity-matched one. Three single-factor variants retain the full PFIM but route its centered importance $\widehat I_t$ to exactly one executable location: \texttt{gamma} feeds it only into Eq.~\ref{eq:gamma}, \texttt{write} only into Eq.~\ref{eq:write}, and \texttt{delta} only into Eq.~\ref{eq:delta}, with the remaining locations held at their neutral values. \texttt{gamma\_write} feeds $\widehat I_t$ into both drive factors but leaves the transition step unadapted. Two parameter-matched controls isolate the spatial structure of the importance field itself: \texttt{global} averages $\widehat I_t$ over all tokens, removing token-wise alignment while preserving the PFIM parameter count, and \texttt{no\_2dstd} keeps token-wise importance but computes local variation from the sequence neighborhood only, dropping the two-dimensional second-moment term. Because $\gamma_t$ and $w_t$ multiply the same scan input in the executable path, the single-factor runs diagnose the factorization of one drive rather than independent recurrent update locations.

\subsection{Executable Selective Scan and Block Output}

All reported experiments use the CUDA fast path. Because the kernel does not expose an external hidden-state damping input, the implemented drive is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u}^{scan}_t=\gamma_t w_t\mathbf{u}_t,
  \label{eq:scaninput}
\end{equation}
followed by
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{y}^{ssm}&=\mathrm{SelectiveScan}(\mathbf{u}^{scan},\Delta,\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}),\\
  \mathbf{y}&=\mathbf{W}_{out}\!\left[(\mathbf{y}^{ssm}+\mathbf{D}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})
  \odot\mathrm{SiLU}(\mathbf{z})\right].
  \label{eq:output}
\end{align}
The scan operands are explicitly cast to FP32 inside an autocast-disabled region. The result is inserted into a pre-normalized residual block with learnable layer scales initialized to $10^{-2}$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{x}'&=\mathbf{x}+\ell_1\odot\method(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{x})),\\
  \mathbf{x}^{out}&=\mathbf{x}'+\ell_2\odot\mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{x}')).
\end{align}
Depthwise filtering, local pooling, PFIM, and selective scan are all linear in token count $N$. Replacing only one shallow stage yields a complete detector with 5.9648M parameters and 13.2079 GFLOPs at 640 input.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Implemented \method{} Forward Pass}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Tokens $\mathbf{x}$ and spatial shape $(H,W)$
\STATE $\bar{\mathbf{x}}\leftarrow\mathrm{DWConv1D}(\mathbf{x})$
\STATE $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}-\lambda_s(\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})$
\STATE $\mathbf{I}\leftarrow\mathrm{PFIM}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{x}},H,W)$
\STATE Project $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ to $\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z},\Delta,\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}$
\STATE Compute $\gamma$ by Eq.~\ref{eq:gamma} and $w$ by Eq.~\ref{eq:write}
\STATE $\mathbf{u}^{scan}\leftarrow\gamma\odot w\odot\mathbf{u}$
\STATE Run FP32 selective scan and residual output in Eq.~\ref{eq:output}
\RETURN Output tokens $\mathbf{y}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and Protocols}

\paragraph{SSDD.}
The primary archive uses SSDD ship detection experiments at 640 input resolution. The strongest recovered ex27 checkpoint is selected by the training validator's composite fitness, rather than by a post-hoc TEST choice. Because these runs were recovered from multiple servers, we retain their original names and distinguish archive evidence from newly controlled experiments.

\paragraph{SAR-ShipDataset.}
The revised dataset contains 39,729 images and 47,416 instances \citep{sarshipdataset,madinet}. Our local seeded split contains 31,783 training, 3,972 validation, and 3,974 test images. The exact sample list used by MaDiNet is not public, so cross-paper numbers are contextual.

\paragraph{SAR-AIRcraft.}
The original dataset has 4,368 images, 16,463 instances, and image sizes from 800 to 1500 pixels \citep{saraircraft,madinet}. Our available Hugging Face copy is a 512$\times$512 overlapping-patch derivative with 13,976/1,923/2,989 train/validation/test patches. Original-image IDs do not overlap across splits, but duplicate object evidence can appear in neighboring patches. We therefore report both patch validation and patch test results and do not claim direct equivalence with MaDiNet's 90.5 \apfifty{}.

\paragraph{OGSOD-1.0.}
The original benchmark was introduced for optical-guided SAR detection and reports 14,665 paired optical--SAR training images plus 3,666 SAR test images \citep{ogsod}. The recovered archive used here contains 18,331 SAR images in supplied train/validation/test JSON files, converted into 14,664/1,834/1,833 YOLO samples. The converted test split has 4,769 valid boxes from three classes: 3,165 bridges, 399 harbors, and 1,205 tanks. We report this as a local three-way protocol rather than the original published split.

\subsection{Metrics and Training Details}

We report precision, recall, \apfifty{}, and \apcoco{}. Literature values taken from MaDiNet use its reported confidence-0.5 protocol. Our Ultralytics evaluations compute AP by sweeping confidence thresholds; displayed precision and recall correspond to the validator's selected operating point. These definitions should not be conflated. For the matched OGSOD runs, training saves \texttt{best.pt} using validation fitness $0.1\,\mathrm{AP}_{50}+0.9\,\mathrm{AP}_{50:95}$; each selected checkpoint is then evaluated once on the held-out local TEST split in FP32. Thus, the TEST values in Table~\ref{tab:ogsod} are not used for checkpoint selection, but one run per architecture is still insufficient to estimate seed variance.

It is not, by itself, evidence of a stable improvement across random seeds.

\section{Discussion}

The strongest controlled single-run evidence is OGSOD-1.0, where the matched T-scale comparison improves AP$_{75}$ and AP$_{50:95}$ without increasing model size. Table~\ref{tab:evidence} deliberately separates this observation from archive deltas. SSDD and SAR-ShipDataset broaden the application coverage, while SAR-Aircraft exposes a severe distribution shift, but none of these archives establish an additional controlled architecture comparison. The T model becomes precision-oriented on aircraft patch validation, obtaining 90.96 precision and 79.63 recall. This behavior is compatible with suppression of ambiguous responses, but controlled threshold and calibration studies are needed before attributing the trade-off to PFIM.

The OGSOD tank result narrows the claim further. Dense, repetitive scattering patterns may require instance-separating cues that are not represented by one shared local importance field. Density-stratified evaluation and importance-map visualization are necessary to determine whether the degradation comes from residual smoothing, shared modulation, or the detector head. We therefore report the observation as a limitation rather than a resolved mechanism.

The comparison with MaDiNet highlights different design goals. MaDiNet obtains higher AP50 through a diffusion detector and a large MambaSAR backbone. \method{} uses one shallow SAR-aware stage inside a 5.96M-parameter one-stage detector. The current evidence therefore supports an accuracy-efficiency claim, not a universal SOTA claim.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility Status}

A matched OGSOD comparison now provides one controlled architecture reference, but four limitations remain. First, it is a single run per model and does not establish variance across seeds. Second, PFIM has not yet been decomposed into gamma-only, write-only, delta-only, no-PFIM, and parameter-matched spatially unstructured controls. These diagnostics are needed to isolate the factorized drive parameterization from transition adaptation; gamma and write should not be treated as distinct operator locations. Third, several SSDD rows come from recovered multi-server experiments; the SAR-Ship baseline changed global batch from 256 to 128; and the available SAR-Aircraft baseline used a different optimizer, batch, and training duration. These remain archive comparisons. Fourth, MaDiNet does not publish exact split files for all three datasets, our aircraft data are overlapping patches, and the recovered OGSOD split differs from the original published protocol.

The controlled matrix needed to close the first two limitations is fully instrumented but not yet executed. A single runner freezes the OGSOD data fingerprint and code/config hashes, records the training protocol, and evaluates each validation-selected \texttt{best.pt} once on TEST in FP32; an aggregator enforces evidence fields and emits per-seed means, standard deviations, and paired deltas, with a strict completion check that refuses to emit a publication table from incomplete seeds. The remaining step is server-side: run the baseline, no-PFIM, global-PFIM, and full PFIM models under the same initialization procedure and assigned seed, optimizer, batch, augmentation, and 300-epoch schedule for seeds 0/1/2, followed by the seed-0 branch diagnostics and density/scale/spacing analysis. All commands, run paths, data fingerprints, code hashes, and per-class metrics used in this draft are preserved in the project reports.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented \method, an importance-guided de-speckle state space module for lightweight SAR target detection. It attenuates local sequence residuals, estimates importance from local spatial statistics, and conditions a factorized selective-scan drive together with the transition step. Integrated into Mamba-YOLO-T, the model remains compact at 5.96M parameters and 13.21 GFLOPs. One matched OGSOD-1.0 run per architecture shows a 1.300-point AP$_{50:95}$ gain with negligible AP$_{50}$ change; three archive datasets provide protocol-limited context rather than a replicated cross-dataset claim. The dense-tank result also shows that the benefit is not uniform across object layouts. The defensible conclusion is therefore specific: \method{} is a lightweight candidate for localization-sensitive SAR detection whose causal benefit remains pending multi-seed, parameter-matched, and branch-level validation.
